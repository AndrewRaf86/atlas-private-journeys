For Serious Travelers

Ayahuasca in Peru

Some travelers come to Peru curious about ayahuasca. Atlas treats it as a serious traditional Amazonian ceremonial practice — not a recreational drug, a party activity, or a casual tourist experience.

Ayahuasca has a long history among Indigenous communities of the Amazon. The brew is traditionally prepared with the Banisteriopsis caapi vine and other Amazonian plants, most commonly chacruna. It has been used for generations in ceremonial and spiritual contexts and should be approached with preparation, honesty, and care.

Atlas Private Journeys does not organize or facilitate ayahuasca ceremonies directly. For guests who are genuinely interested and want to research it further, we may point them toward dedicated retreat centers that focus on proper preparation, participant screening, experienced facilitation, and post-ceremony integration.

Dreamglade Retreat

One option for serious travelers is Dreamglade, a small ayahuasca retreat near Iquitos, Peru. Known for a quiet rainforest setting, small-group ceremonies, and traditional Shipibo-led facilitation. Not affiliated with Atlas — we mention it as a starting point for your own research.

Learn About Dreamglade

Important: Ayahuasca is not appropriate for everyone. Before considering any ceremony, guests should be fully honest about their medical history, current medications, mental health background, and substance use. A reputable retreat should screen participants carefully before accepting them.

A Respectful Referral, Not a Party Add-On

Atlas focuses on travel logistics, private transportation, curated stays, and local coordination. Ayahuasca is a separate subject that deserves its own careful consideration — it should not be treated as an entertainment option or mixed into casual trip planning.

If this path interests you, take time to research, ask questions directly to any retreat you consider, and consult a doctor if relevant.
