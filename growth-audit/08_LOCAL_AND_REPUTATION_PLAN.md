# Local and Reputation Plan

Research date: 2026-07-11. Google Business Profile policy confirmed against current official Google Business Profile Help documentation (see Sources).

## Does Atlas qualify for a Google Business Profile?

**Yes — as a Service-Area Business (SAB).** Confirmed against current Google policy: businesses that travel to customers (rather than hosting them at a fixed storefront) are eligible, provided the in-person service genuinely happens and the listing doesn't use a virtual office or PO box as an address. This matches Atlas exactly: Fidel meets clients in Lima (airport, hotels, around the city) rather than at a Atlas-owned location.

Setup requirements, per Google's current guidance:
- Leave the public-facing address blank/hidden (do not display a fake or virtual address).
- Set the service area to Lima (Google's own guidance caps a service area at roughly a 2-hour drive from the business's base — Lima-only is well within this).
- Complete phone/postcard or video verification tied to the actual business owner — **this step requires Andrew or Fidel's direct participation; I cannot complete verification on their behalf.**

## Why this matters more than almost anything else in this plan

Cross-referencing `04_SEARCH_VISIBILITY_BASELINE.md`: Atlas currently has zero presence anywhere Google or a prospective client would look to corroborate it's real. A verified GBP listing is free, gets discovered independently of the website's own indexing status, and is often crawled/surfaced faster than a small static site. It's the single highest-leverage action in this entire plan that costs nothing but setup time.

## Review-acquisition system

Atlas has zero reviews on any external platform today. The three existing testimonials (Jay, Ram B., Sam) were collected via a private form — good raw material, but **their existence on the website is not the same as a public, platform-verified review**, which is what actually builds third-party trust and what Google/other users can independently check.

**Sequencing (do not skip step 1):**
1. Confirm consent from Jay, Ram B., and Sam to be contacted about leaving a public review (Owner Decision — I will not contact them without this).
2. Once GBP is live and verified, request a Google review from each of the three past clients, plus every future client at trip completion.
3. Timing: request within 24-48 hours of trip completion, when the experience is freshest — not immediately at a high-emotion moment (e.g., not mid-trip) and not so late the client has moved on mentally.

**Draft review-request WhatsApp message** (for Andrew/Fidel's use, not to be sent by me):
> "Hi [name] — really glad you had a good time in Lima. If you have two minutes, a Google review would help other travelers find Atlas the way you did: [GBP review link]. Thank you either way!"

**Draft review-request email** (for use once a contact email exists):
> Subject: Quick favor after your Lima trip
> "Hi [name], hope you're settling back in after Lima. If Fidel's help made a difference, a short Google review would mean a lot and helps other travelers find us: [link]. No pressure, and thanks again for choosing Atlas."

**Response protocol:**
- Positive reviews: reply publicly, specific, brief — thank them and reference something real from their trip (not a generic template).
- Negative reviews: reply calmly, acknowledge the specific issue, take detail offline via WhatsApp/email, never argue publicly. Given Atlas is a single-operator business, a negative review is also a real signal worth acting on, not just managing.
- Never gate reviews (i.e., never ask happy clients to review publicly while routing unhappy clients to a private form only) — this violates Google's review policies and is explicitly excluded from this plan.
- Turning reviews into case studies: only with explicit written permission per person, matching the same consent standard already required for the existing testimonials.
