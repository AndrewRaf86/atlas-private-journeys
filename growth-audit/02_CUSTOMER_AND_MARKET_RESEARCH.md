# Customer and Market Research

Evidence sources: existing testimonials (the only direct customer evidence Atlas has), the three built service pages (which reflect deliberate prior prioritization), and competitor-landscape research (`03_COMPETITOR_GAP_ANALYSIS.md`).

## What the existing evidence actually shows about who books Atlas today

- **Ram B.'s testimonial**: "Our group of 10 guys" — a bachelor/friend-group trip, Lima-based, clearly a group logistics use case.
- **Jay's testimonial**: mentions "tour guides" (plural) and "Airbnb location" — suggests a group or multi-person trip with accommodation coordination touching Atlas's scope.
- **Sam's testimonial**: references a specific cultural site (Huaca Pucllana) and a restaurant — reads as a smaller-group or couple guide-day experience.

This is a small sample (three data points) but it's Atlas's **only real evidence**, and it consistently points toward **groups and pairs doing a bounded, specific Lima experience** — not solo backpacker research-heavy trips, and not large agency-style multi-week package buyers.

## Recommended focus

**Primary: bachelor/friend groups and couples/small-group guide days, both Lima-only.** These are the two segments with direct evidence, existing dedicated pages, and comparatively thin direct competition.

**Secondary: airport transfer as an entry point** — lower value alone, but a natural way for a first-time Atlas visitor to start a relationship that becomes a guide day or repeat booking, and it's genuinely underserved by personal-guide-style competitors (the space is dominated by anonymous transport companies).

**Avoid for now**: broad first-time-Peru researchers and luxury-positioned affluent travelers — both require either content/proof Atlas doesn't have yet (multi-region itineraries, luxury credentials) or would compete directly against entrenched Tier 1 agencies with no real differentiation to offer.

## Segment-specific persuasion notes

**Bachelor/group segment:**
- Main fear: a chaotic, badly-coordinated group weekend, or being overcharged as an obvious tourist group.
- Most persuasive offer: single point of contact for the whole group's logistics (already the core pitch on `/lima-bachelor-party`).
- Trust signal needed most: proof it's been done before for a similar-sized group — Ram B.'s "group of 10" testimonial is doing real work here and should be kept prominent.
- Planning timeline: typically weeks, not months (matches the existing FAQ answer "4-8 weeks out" on that page).

**Couples/small-group guide-day segment:**
- Main fear: an impersonal, rushed, checklist-style tour.
- Most persuasive offer: flexible, personal, locally-informed pacing — already the core pitch on `/private-guide-lima`.
- Trust signal needed most: specific, credible local knowledge (the Huaca Pucllana + restaurant detail in Sam's testimonial is exactly this kind of proof and should stay prominent).
